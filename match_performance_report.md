Wales’ 31–17 bonus-point win over Italy showed the benefit of a settled starting XV: the unchanged side built a 31–0 lead through coherent set-piece execution and sharper attacking intent. Aaron Wainwright was the principal gain-line threat, scoring twice and adding 24 metres, four defenders beaten and a clean break from 13 carries; his output converted midfield momentum into points. Captain Dewi Lake also finished a lineout drive and secured all 11 of Wales’ recorded lineout wins, giving the attack a reliable platform. Dan Edwards’ 16-point contribution—one try, four conversions and a drop-goal—underlined growing confidence and more ambitious kicking after his return to the side.

The back three and half-back combination helped turn pressure into territory and scoring opportunities. Louis Rees-Zammit made 46 metres and beat three defenders, while the report credits his chase pressure with helping create the opening penalty and disrupting Italy’s defence. Ellis Mee paired 31 metres and three defenders beaten with six tackles, and his late effort denied Monty Ioane; that all-round work rate merits reinforcing. Edwards added a clean break and three defenders beaten, while scrum-half Tom Williams’ 71 passes and one try assist point to a high-tempo distribution role. The aerial contest was a clear early vulnerability, however: Italy were superior in the opening quarter, so review back-three spacing, chase coordination and contest support without losing the pressure that helped unlock Wales’ attack.

The main performance-management concern is sustaining control after a commanding start. Italy scored 17 unanswered points in the second half, including while Wales were down to 14 after Archie Griffin’s 52nd-minute yellow card; Griffin also missed two tackles in five and conceded a penalty. Across the starting side, Ellis Mee conceded four turnovers, while defensive misses included two each for Rees-Zammit, Hawkins and Botham—worth targeted review alongside the late defensive sequences, even as Botham’s 16 tackles without a miss and the report’s praise for Wales’ resistance show strong individual commitment. For the next review, prioritise short-handed defensive organisation, cleaner possession and exit decisions under pressure, and penalty discipline; preserve the lineout-driven attack and Edwards’ assertive game management that produced the dominant first-half platform.
